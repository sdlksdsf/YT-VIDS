# Our Style Bible — "Echoes of Empires"

> The hybrid style we will use for every full-length historical video. It combines the proven mechanics of the 5 reference channels into one ownable, repeatable system.

---

## 1. Brand idea

**Working name:** Echoes of Empires
**Tagline:** *History that hits like a movie.*
**Promise:** One event. One story. Told like a film — real research, hand-made art, human voice, zero boredom.

**The one-line pitch:** We take the single most dramatic turning point in history and tell it as a ticking-clock movie, with cartoon characters, living maps, and a narrator who feels like a friend.

## 2. The signature device: "The Countdown"

Every episode is built around a **ticking clock**. The title and the chapter cards count down:

- "The Last 53 Days of Rome"
- Chapter cards: `DAY 1`, `DAY 12`, `DAY 22`, `DAY 53`
- A small on-screen countdown chip in the corner during the siege acts.

This gives us what the references each have separately: Unknown Frequencies' escalation, Historically's chapter discs, Epic History's campaign spine, and OverSimplified's character comedy — unified by a countdown that creates urgency.

**Secondary device — "Two Thrones":** for events with two clear leaders, cut between both sides of the same day. The viewer sees the same 24 hours from the palace and from the trenches. This is our ownable twist.

## 3. Visual system

### 3.1 Character kit (OverSimplified-style, upgraded)
- One base body: big head, expressive eyebrows/mouth, no nose, simple hands.
- Identity through **hair, headwear, uniform, and one prop**: Mehmed = turban + red cloak + compass/map; Constantine = crown + purple cloak + sword; Giustiniani = Genoese cross + helmet; Orban = apron + hammer.
- Characters have **one running gag each** (Mehmed always studying a map; Constantine always touching the walls; Orban always polishing his cannon).
- Consistent faces across the whole video = instant recognition.

### 3.2 Maps (Epic History-style, animated)
- Parchment-and-ink base map, modern clean labels.
- Animated arrows for troop movement, pulsing dots for armies, fire for destruction.
- "You are here" camera: every location change starts with a zoom from a wide map to the exact spot.
- On-screen labels for every name, date, and place mentioned.

### 3.3 Lighting & color script
| Moment | Palette | Lighting |
|---|---|---|
| Constantinople at peace | Gold, warm stone, blue sea | Late-afternoon sun |
| The Ottoman camp | Red, black, dust | Torchlight, cold dawn |
| The cannon | Orange muzzle flash, grey smoke | High contrast, silhouette |
| The Golden Horn chain | Deep blue, silver | Night, moonlight |
| The last service | Candle gold, purple | Flickering, intimate |
| The final assault | Grey → red | Overcast, fire glow |
| The aftermath | Ash grey → sunrise | Cold → warm |

### 3.4 Captions & kinetic type
- Big, bold, high-contrast captions timed to the voice (never walls of text).
- Key numbers get a **stat card**: "53 DAYS", "7,000 vs 80,000", "600 KG STONE BALL".
- Names appear with a small portrait icon the first time.
- Quotes appear as handwritten-style text over the scene.

### 3.5 Camera language
- Slow push-in before a reveal.
- Quick whip-pan on a surprise.
- Top-down "map view" for strategy.
- Over-the-shoulder "you are there" for immersion.
- Hold on a wide shot in silence after a huge moment.

## 4. Audio system

- **Narrator:** one consistent human voice (warm, witty, cinematic — not a robot, not a salesman). Same voice across the whole channel.
- **Music:** Kevin MacLeod (CC-BY) + YouTube Audio Library + Free Music Archive. Three beds: `tension`, `tragedy`, `triumph`. Music ducks under narration.
- **SFX:** cannon boom, chain rattle, crowd roar, whistle, horses, fire, church bells, choir. Freesound.org (CC0/CC-BY) + YouTube Audio Library.
- **Silence:** 1-2 seconds of near-silence before the final assault and before the reveal of the emperor's fate.
- **Sound motifs:** the cannon boom = Mehmed's theme; the church bell = Constantine's theme; the chain rattle = the city's theme.

## 5. Script structure (the 6-beat spine)

1. **Cold open (0:00-0:45):** the most dramatic moment of the story, told as a teaser. Then: "But it didn't start that way."
2. **The world (0:45-4:00):** the state of things, the stakes, the two sides. End with the decision that starts the clock.
3. **The build (4:00-9:00):** plans, weapons, characters, obstacles. Mini-cliffhanger: the first major reversal.
4. **The struggle (9:00-16:00):** the middle act — escalating failures and small victories. Each 2-3 minutes ends with a "but then…".
5. **The climax (16:00-20:00):** the final day. Fast cuts, countdown, peak music, the decisive moment.
6. **The echo (20:00-22:00):** aftermath + legacy + the line that reframes the whole story. End with a question to the audience.

**Pacing rules:**
- One idea per scene. Scene length 20-90 seconds.
- A joke, a fact, or a twist every 15-25 seconds.
- Never explain a joke. Never over-explain the obvious.
- If a sentence doesn't move the story or deepen a character, cut it.

## 6. Title / thumbnail / description formulas

**Title formulas (rotate):**
- `The Last __ Days of __: __`
- `The ENTIRE History of __ Explained`
- `__ vs __: The __ That Changed Everything`
- `__ [No AI]` (use only if we truly make it by hand)

**Thumbnail formula:** one face (the hero or villain) + one huge word or number + one bold color + one visual hook (cannon, chain, fire). Same layout every video = instant channel recognition.

**Description formula:**
1. One-line hook.
2. 2-3 sentence story summary.
3. Timestamps (chapters).
4. Sources & further reading.
5. Credits (music, art, voice).
6. CTA (subscribe + comment question).

## 7. The "no-boredom" checklist (run before every publish)

- [ ] First 10 seconds contain a shocking fact or a question.
- [ ] Every 2-4 minutes has a scene change, joke, or twist.
- [ ] Every named character has a visual hook and a motive.
- [ ] Every map change shows where we are.
- [ ] Every chapter ends on a mini-cliffhanger.
- [ ] The climax has a countdown and a silence beat.
- [ ] The ending answers the hook and adds a legacy.
- [ ] No paragraph is longer than ~4 sentences of narration.
- [ ] No joke is explained; no obvious thing is over-explained.
- [ ] Captions, numbers, and names are on screen.
- [ ] Human voice, human art, real sources.

## 8. What makes it *ours* (not a copy)

1. **The Countdown** — a ticking clock on screen in every episode.
2. **Two Thrones** — cutting between both leaders' same day.
3. **A hand-made, human-voiced promise** — explicitly "no AI slop".
4. **A legacy ending** that connects the event to the modern world (why *you* care).
5. **A consistent character kit and map language** that builds channel recognition.
